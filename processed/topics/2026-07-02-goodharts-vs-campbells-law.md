While both Goodhart's Law and Campbell's Law address the breakdown of quantitative metrics when used as targets, they differ in framing and emphasis:

- **Goodhart's Law (Statistical / Information-Theoretic Framing)**: Focuses on how a proxy metric loses statistical validity and predictive power once it becomes an explicit target of optimization.
- **Campbell's Law (Behavioral / Institutional Framing)**: Focuses on the corrupting incentive pressures exerted on human actors and social processes within a system when metrics determine decisions.

### Synthesis & Overlap
- **Campbell's Case**: Standardized test score optimization ("teaching to the test") exemplifies Campbell's Law by distorting educational practices and human incentives.
- **Goodhart's Case**: Optimizing software developer output via lines-of-code metrics exemplifies Goodhart's Law by breaking the metric's correlation with value.
- **AI Systems Context**: AI reward hacking represents a pure algorithmic instance of Goodhart's Law, where optimization against a target reward signal causes divergence from intended behavior without human behavioral corruption.
