Campbell's Law states: "The more any quantitative social indicator is used for social decision-making, the more subject it will be to corruption pressures and the more apt it will be to distort and corrupt the social processes it is intended to monitor."

Coined by social scientist Donald T. Campbell, this law highlights how heavy reliance on quantitative metrics in human systems creates incentive pressures that corrupt both the metric and the human behavior behind it.

### Classic Examples
- **High-Stakes Testing**: In education, evaluating schools or teachers based primarily on standardized test scores leads to "teaching to the test" or outright metric manipulation, undermining overall educational quality.
